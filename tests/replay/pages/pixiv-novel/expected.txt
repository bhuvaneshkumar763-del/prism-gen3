Among the Crystal Tribe, who live exposed to the miasma, there were some who were born with crystallized eyeballs once every few decades. Their crystallization began from the inside, and if left untreated, they would die as babies. Therefore, their lifespan was extended by exposing them sufficiently to the miasma to accelerate crystallization from the outside, and only after their bodies had grown large enough were they sent down to the Land of Eternal Night. The deadline was sixteen years, which was the age when Almeo and his companions were considered to have reached adulthood.
When Armeo saw his sister for the first time, he was breathless at her beauty and at the same time began to question his own fate. As the next chieftain, Armeo had been raised in a strict but warm environment, cherished by those around him. However, it was unbearable for Armeo to know that his sister, who was born of the same blood, had never known parental love or loneliness since birth, and had only been constantly exposed to the cold miasma.
What pained Armeo the most was that she was simply called a "sacrifice." He understood the logic that giving a name would create affection. But even so, the mere thought that the girl sitting there was himself torn apart Armeo's heart.
So Armeo would often sneak away from his father's watchful eye to visit the "offering." If she couldn't defy her fate of descending to the land of eternal night when she turned sixteen, then Armeo wanted to give her at least a little bit of human dignity before then. That was Armeo's wish.

Children born with crystallized eyes were blind. To help the "offering," who couldn't perceive colors, understand, Armeo told them all sorts of stories from outside the palace. The "offering" greatly enjoyed Armeo's stories and laughed with a voice that sparkled like glass.

"Sunlight is necessary for a good scent."
"Is that so? I'd like to experience the scent of sunshine someday."
The "offering," who had never known light since birth, murmured innocently. The "offering" knew that soon she would go to the land of eternal night where even moonlight could not reach. Standing before the transparent girl with the same face as himself, Almeo felt half of his body grow cold each time he recalled that fact.
"Brother, it seems someone has arrived."
Since the "offering" was blind, its hearing was exceptional. When Armeo turned around, a figure covered in white fur was standing behind him.
"Hey Armeo. I thought you'd come out today since there's no wind."
"I didn't expect you to come out here too, Jega."
The one who appeared before Almeo was Jega, the next chieftain of the Eternal Night Tribe. Covered in pure white fur, the Eternal Night Tribe were disliked for their appearance, and even among the Crystal Tribe, more people found them eerie. Living in the darkness where sunlight does not reach, the Eternal Night Tribe had no eyesight from the beginning, and their fur was depigmented because they rarely exposed to light. Instead, they understood their surroundings through heat sensing, smell, and a special kind of hearing.
From a young age, Almeo accompanied his father on trades with the Eternal Night tribe. The trade included not only night crystals, but also other rare minerals and goods found in the land of the Eternal Night. Almeo learned the ins and outs of trading from his father, and it was there that he met Jega, who was also learning about trading. At first, he found the Eternal Night tribe, with their fur that shone white like quartz powder, unsettling, but Almeo soon realized that Jega was a bright young boy.
From then on, Armeo began to hold Jega in high regard. Armeo respected the boy who lived in a world different from his own, and Jega grew fond of Armeo, who, despite being human, had become attached to him.
"But what exactly do you want?"
"What's wrong with coming to check on my precious bride?"
With that lighthearted remark, Jega approached the "offering" and held out his hand. Even without directly touching it, he could sense the "offering" through his touch.
"Calling me a bride sounds terrible. If that happened, you and I would become brothers-in-law."
"That's fine. It would be wonderful if a member of the Night Tribe and a human became family."
Almeo stared at Jega. The fur that protected him from the miasma covered what appeared to be his face with long hair, so he couldn't see Jega's expression. Almeo reminded himself that the Night Tribe did not rely on their eyesight to live, and told himself that he would never see Jega's face. Jega raised his hand over the "offering" again and spoke.
"You feel the same way, don't you? We'll take good care of you from now on, so you have nothing to worry about. That's right, the Land of Eternal Night is much warmer than here. The piercingly cold light doesn't pour down. The darkness will protect us."
Almeo's heart began to pound at the words. Jega described moonlight as "cold" and darkness as "warm." Almeo had mistakenly assumed that cold and dark were unpleasant things, but he realized that for Jega, the environment outside the cave was actually more difficult to live in.
Jega had been holding his hand over the "offering" for a while, then suddenly turned to look at Armeo.
"A strong night wind will soon be blowing. You should go home too."
The wind-reading abilities of the Tokoya tribe are accurate. Having learned this from his father, Almeo decided to follow Jega. Saying goodbye to the "offering," Almeo and Jega left the palace. After walking together on the quartz for a while, Jega spoke.
"Hey, I think we should have stopped doing this."
"What are you talking about?"
"You understand, don't you? What happens to a crystallized human being?"
Of course, Almeo knew. The "offerings" placed in the Land of Eternal Night were exposed to an even stronger miasma, causing their crystallized bodies to recrystallize further, eventually transforming into large night crystals. Night crystals were the remains of Eternal Night tribesmen exposed to the miasma, or the remains of fully crystallized humans. Night crystals formed from crystallized humans were particularly valuable. While people lamented the birth of a child with transparent eyes, they tended to rejoice when high-quality night crystals could be traded at high prices.
Jega realized that Armeo felt sibling affection for the "offerings." To Jega, it seemed cruel to instill such feelings in them, knowing they were destined to be turned to stone and shattered.
"I know. But I wonder what would happen if I were the one to turn into the Night Crystal."
"But you won't become the Night Crystal."
"It's just a hypothetical situation. It was entirely possible. It just so happened that she was chosen instead of me, and it wouldn't be strange at all if I were sitting in that spot. If I were to turn into stone... I'd want to remain forever, holding onto beautiful memories."
Armeo couldn't see Jega's expression, but he could sense that Jega was somewhat exasperated with him.
"Oh, really... Is that so? That's just your ego, isn't it? You're quite the romantic."
"No, it's not. She is me, and I am her. That's what it means to be born of the same blood."
"I don't understand. I thought humans were creatures with fewer emotions."
"What do you mean by that?"
Then Jega spread his arms wide as if he had been waiting for this and continued speaking.
"I've always thought it was strange that humans valued night crystals and other stones so highly. After all, they're all the corpses of living things. Quartz is the same. You guys catch animals from other places, expose them to miasma, turn them all into stone, and sell them. What's the point of doing that?"
Armeo couldn't answer Jega's question. It would have been easy to say that they sold the stones in exchange for food and building materials. But that wouldn't have adequately explained to Jega the human desire for stones.
"Well, you know... we might be far more cruel than you can understand."
Seeing how dejected Almeo was, Jega hastily tried to smooth things over.
"Don't take it so seriously. I finally let go of my mother's night crystal the other day. If it means something good will come into the village, I'm sure my mother would be happy."
"That's it."
Armeo instinctively took Jega's hand.
"I want that child to never forget me, even when she turns to stone. Yes, I'm sure that's it. Isn't that why you think of the crystal at night and your mother together?"
As Armeo ran his fingers through Jega's fur beneath his glove, he longed to touch its pure white hair directly. He thought that if he did, he might be able to convey to Jega the feelings he couldn't put into words.
"Hmm, I feel like something's not quite right... but let's just go with that."
Jega turned his head and moved away from Armeo.
"Well then, I'll be taking my leave now. We'll meet again on the day of the descent."
"Ah, well then... hey, would you do me just one thing? It's very simple. Could you move the hair off your face and turn it towards me?"
Armeo pleaded with Jega. Jega, noticing it was nothing significant and not particularly important, looked puzzled and ran his fingers through the hair on his face.
"Humans are really strange, aren't they... Is this okay? Ugh, the night air is cold."
Armeo saw Jega's face, now exposed. He felt that the "answer" he had been searching for was there.
"……thank you"
Jega quickly let down the hair on his face. Then, after a brief farewell, Armeo headed home to the village outside the forest, and Jega went home to the land of eternal night where miasma raged, both treading through the quartz dust.
Soon, just as Jega had predicted, a strong night wind blew. The trees, imbued with the miasma, froze again, and quartz powder glittered in the wind. No one witnessed the beautiful sight; only the "offering" in the transparent palace of the glass forest listened intently to the sound of the wind.

His father, the chief, took Almeo and a few attendants to the palace. Inside the palace were the chief of the Eternal Night tribe, Jega, and several other members of the Eternal Night tribe.
"Effective today, we hereby transfer responsibility for the handling of this ore."
The contract was read aloud, and the "offering" was lowered from its pedestal. It was then placed in a vehicle brought by the Eternal Night tribe and began to quietly head towards the Land of Eternal Night.
"Just wait a moment."
After the ceremony was over, Almeo addressed the Night Tribe.
"Stop it, she belongs to them now. She's not your sister."
Ignoring his father's pleas to stop, Armeo headed not for the "offering," but for Jega, who was accompanying him. Then, Armeo took a yellow fruit from his pocket.
"...Please offer this to her. It's a fruit filled with sunshine, bought from a merchant from a tropical country. I want you to be her light in my place."

"...I understand how you feel. It must be painful to lose half of yourself, but it's also painful to watch your daughter die. But that is our fate. This is what it means to live with the night wind and the stones."

"...Let's go. The night wind has gotten stronger. You're going back to your own world."
Armeo was led back to the village by his father, weeping. It was unclear whether those tears stemmed from grief over the loss of his sister, or from some other emotion.

Time passed quickly, and it was time for Armeo to step down after assuming the chieftainship. Having married a village girl and had several children, Armeo was passing on to his eldest son the wisdom he had learned from his own father about how to survive in the Glass Forest as chieftain.
Almeo had witnessed two infants with crystallized eyes. Following the rules, Almeo kept them in his palace for sixteen years before calmly handing them over to the Eternal Night Tribe. Jega, who had grown old with Almeo as the chief of the Eternal Night Tribe, teased the calm Almeo the second time he took them over, saying, "You've gotten old too."
It happened during a regular transaction with the Eternal Night tribe. Jega said, "I want to speak with your chief alone," and sent their respective sons, who were accompanying them, back home. Eventually, when only Jega and Almeo's breathing could be heard in the glass forest, Jega finally spoke.
"...It's been a long time since we've talked like this, just the two of us."
His voice was not the dignified tone of a chieftain as usual, but had the resonance of the time they had spoken under the moonlight long ago. Before Almeo could ask for an explanation, Jega pulled out a night crystal the size of an adult's fist from beneath his fur.
"What is this night crystal...it's an extraordinary object I've never seen before."
The moment Almeo saw the crystal that night, he couldn't sit still. The night crystal, which shimmered as if it held the depths of the night, stirred an uncontrollable turmoil in Almeo's heart.
"Listen carefully, no, look carefully."
At Jega's words, Almeo picked up the night crystal and held it up to the moonlight. The light shone through the night crystal with a blinding brightness that seemed to burn into Almeo's eyes, but at the same time, Almeo noticed a large shadow in the night crystal. When he looked closely, it seemed as if something was embedded inside.
"...Is that...fruit peel?"
At that moment, the events of that day flashed through Almeo's mind. His sister who had turned invisible, his sister who could not understand light and his friend from the Night Tribe, his own powerlessness to do anything but submit to fate, and the vivid scent of lemons brought from the land of eternal summer. "Even after placing your sister in the depths of the night... in a place with a thick miasma, it takes quite a while for her to completely crystallize. During that time, your sister wanted this fruit, so I let her hold it. She said, 'It smells like sunshine,' and she didn't let go until she stopped reacting. My father was angry at the time, saying that the value of the night crystal would be lost, but what do you think?"
The night crystal containing the lemon fragment shone more beautifully in Armeo's eyes than any other night crystal.
"Lemons are no problem at all. What is this sparkle? I've never seen anything like it."
"Right? That got me thinking. Why is this night crystal so captivating?"
Jega paused for a moment, then continued.
"This crystal is alive tonight. Stones hold the feelings of the people who were there before they turned to stone. That's why humans desire stones, isn't it? You'll never let go of this stone again, will you?"
"Yes, of course not. I've finally come back."
Armeo held the night crystal he had been holding to his chest and showed it to the moonlight.
"Hey, can you see? That's moonlight. When morning comes, I'll take you to see the sunlight. When I step down as chief, we'll go to a tropical country. There, we'll soak up the scent of the sun."
Seeing Armeo in tears, Jega remained silent for a while. Then, he slowly rose to his feet and prepared to return to the Land of Eternal Night.
"Wait a minute, you'll be stepping down as chieftain soon, won't you? When that happens, why don't you join me on this journey? I know the Eternal Night Tribe isn't accepted by humans. But I want to get to know you a little better. Not the Eternal Night Tribe, but you yourself."
Armeo shouted, and Jega responded by raising his hand without turning around.
"Unfortunately, but I'll accept your feelings. I think feelings are what's important for stones. With your feelings, I feel like I can become an even more wonderful night crystal than my mother. I want to take you to the bottom of the night. All sorts of stones emit strong feelings... like, 'I'm so lonely, so lonely.'"
I sensed the night breeze beginning to blow in the distance. Jega continued in a loud voice.
"Perhaps the night breeze is the overwhelming emotion of someone from long ago. It cries out loudly, 'I'm lonely, I'm lonely,' trying to make the living its companions. That's the conclusion I've come to after continuing to work with stones... I can't tell anyone else this, though."
Hearing this, Armeo chuckled. Then he stood up and shouted towards Jega's back.
"You've become quite sentimental, haven't you? We've both gotten older."
Jega's fur swayed. Armeo no longer cared what expression Jega had on his face.
"Yes. I'll seriously consider going to the tropical country."
"If it means descending into the depths of night with you, I'd be happy to go."
Jega waved in response to Almeo's voice and returned to the Land of Eternal Night. Watching him go, Almeo wondered why both humans and the people of Eternal Night remained so fascinated by stones.
On his way home, Armeo held his sister's Night Crystal in his arms and recalled the face of Jega that he had seen before. Where the eyes, which were unnecessary for the Night Tribe, had once been, two Night Crystals shone, like remnants of organs that had once been there.
